# Domain: Team Health Metrics

## Purpose

Team health metrics show whether the team can keep up its pace without burning out. They often change before delivery numbers do. A team that is quietly overloaded may hit normal throughput for months, then drop sharply.

Collect them with care. People answer honestly only when they trust how the data is used. Share the purpose, keep individual responses private, and report only group-level results.

## Engagement

- **Measure:** a short pulse survey, 3 to 5 questions, sent every two to four weeks.
- **Example questions:** "I have what I need to do good work." "I can say no to work without consequences." "I expect to be here in a year."
- **Report:** the share who agree, with the trend. Ignore changes of one or two responses.
- **Action:** pick one item the team can change. Report back on it next cycle.

## Workload

- **Work in progress per person.** High values mean context switching. Compare with the team's WIP limit.
- **Share of time on unplanned work.** Interrupts, incidents, and urgent requests. Above about 20 to 25 percent, planned work usually slips.
- **Overtime and weekend commits.** Count commits and deploys outside working hours, grouped at team level. A rising trend is a warning even if nobody complains.

## On-call load

- **Pages per on-call shift.** Pages during the week and at night, reported separately.
- **Hours of sleep disruption.** Alerts between 22:00 and 07:00 local time.
- **Alert quality.** Share of alerts that needed action. If most alerts need no action, people stop trusting them.

Rotate on-call fairly and review the load at each sprint retrospective.

## Retention

- **Attrition rate.** Leavers over the last 12 months, divided by average headcount. Track regretted leavers separately.
- **Internal moves.** People moving to another team can signal either growth or dissatisfaction. Ask why.
- **Time since last promotion or role change.** Long gaps are a common reason for departure.

Attrition is a lagging signal. By the time it rises, the warning signs were there months earlier, usually in engagement and workload.

## Leading signals to watch

- Pulse scores falling for two cycles in a row
- Fewer people taking time off, or time off being cancelled
- Growing gap between planned and actual work
- Short, one-line answers in retrospectives where people used to write more

## What to do with the numbers

Use team health data in one-to-one conversations and team retrospectives. Ask open questions first: "What got harder this sprint?" Then match the answer to the data. Do not present individual survey results or on-call counts in performance discussions.
